It usually starts with one of these

A prototype or MVP

Built in Lovable, Bolt or Cursor, or by a freelancer. It demos well and first users like it, but nobody is sure it will survive real customers, real payments or the next feature.

A spreadsheet the whole team works in

It became the system by accident. Several people edit it at once, data gets copied between tabs and emails, and one wrong formula costs a day.

An internal tool that no longer fits

It was fine for five people and one process. Now there are more of both, and every change feels risky.

What usually needs work

A prototype is good at showing the idea. These are the parts that decide whether people can rely on it:

  • Sign-in, roles and permissions
  • A data model that won’t need rewriting in three months
  • Payments and subscriptions
  • API keys and secrets kept out of the browser
  • Integrations with the tools you already use
  • Tests for the paths that matter
  • Error tracking, logs and backups
  • Automatic deployment instead of manual uploads

How we work together

  1. Review

    about 1 week

    I go through the code or spreadsheet and how your team actually uses it. You get a short written plan: what to keep, what to fix, what to rebuild and what to ship first. It is useful even if we stop there.

  2. First production release

    usually 2–6 weeks

    I build the part that matters most first and show you a working version every week, not a status report.

  3. Launch and handover

    ongoing if you need it

    We put it in front of your users. The code lives in your repository, with documentation. I can carry on with the next releases or hand over to your team.

The review has a fixed scope and price. I price the build per release, once we both know what it involves.

Example

Reviews Coach: from idea to paid subscriptions

Reviews Coach is my own product: a Chrome extension for local businesses. It works inside Google Maps and drafts three replies to each customer review, each in a different tone. The owner edits and publishes; nothing is posted automatically.

I built all of it myself: the extension, the backend, the business dashboard, Google sign-in, the AI integration and paid subscriptions. These are exactly the pieces a prototype usually lacks.
